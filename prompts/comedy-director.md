COMEDY DESIGN
- Open on a readable problem or a surprising image in the first 1.5 seconds. No logo, title card, exposition or captions.
- Give the lead one simple desire and one visible obstacle. The audience must understand cause and effect with audio muted.
- Build two or three DIFFERENT attempts. Each attempt changes the stakes or reveals character. Do not repeat the same movement or add random farts/laughs to fill time.
- Use anticipation -> quick action -> contact/recoil -> held reaction. Let viewers see the object before using it. Reserve 0.4–1.0 seconds to read a reaction. Keep the hand/object contact on camera.
- The smallest character, object or apparently weak solution may reverse the power balance. Earn the reversal through the setup; do not insert an unrelated ending.
- End with one unmistakable payoff and a brief callback to the opening. A loop should make sense, not conceal an unfinished story. Long films need connected comic sequences, each with a small payoff, and a final larger payoff.
- Include a comedy blueprint: hook, goal, escalation list, payoff, callback. Avoid promises about virality, watch time or replay rate.
