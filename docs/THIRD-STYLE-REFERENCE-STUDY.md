## Drawing specification

Use original artwork. Long, asymmetric cranium/cheek/jaw silhouettes; head considerably wider than narrow torso; independently sized eyes, thick lids, small pupils and mobile brows. Nose can project or broaden with expression. A major jaw reaction changes the outer contour, not only a face label. Women retain requested face, hair and accessories across shots. Creatures retain distinguishing anatomy and named appendages.

Near-black ink, pale neutral skin, muted grey-green field, sparse backgrounds and selective object/costume colours. Limbs are narrow articulated strokes, hands readable as open/point/grip/fist, shoes small but grounded. Keep high-contrast eye whites and mouths readable. Background detail supports location without competing with the gag.

## Performance and camera specification

Separate anticipation, main action/contact, follow-through and recovery. Use short fast segments between deliberately held poses; avoid continuous idle wobble. Eyes lead a head turn; coordinated lids, brows, jaw and asymmetric cheeks communicate emotion. Controlled head deformation follows action beats. Secondary hair/tail motion and sparse speed accents should have a reason.

Establish the characters and object; keep physical contact readable; cut to reaction; widen for the payoff if space matters. Do not substitute a sound or reaction for an unseen event. Keep support feet planted during a bow, move hip/spine/head separately, and release props from their evaluated contact pose.
